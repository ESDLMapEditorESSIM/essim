# Market Prices and Carrier Price Profiles

ESSIM (Energy System Simulator) uses a market-based approach to balance energy supply and demand within energy carrier networks. This document describes how the software works with market prices, price profiles on carriers, and how they influence the simulation behavior.

## Market Configuration

A simulation in ESSIM can be configured with a `Market` object, which defines the price domain and precision for the transport solver. The following properties of the market can be configured in the simulation request:

- **`minPrice`**: The minimum possible price in the market (default: 0.0).
- **`maxPrice`**: The maximum possible price in the market (default: 1.0).
- **`deltaPrice`**: The minimum price step or precision (default: 0.000001).

These boundaries define the coordinate system for all **Bid Curves** generated by assets in the simulation. All prices and marginal costs used in the simulation MUST fall within this range.

## Carrier Price Profiles

In ESDL, an `EnergyCarrier` can have a `cost` profile (of type `GenericProfile`). ESSIM treats these profiles as **exogenous prices**.

### Role of Carrier Price Profiles

1.  **Conversion Viability Check**: For `Conversion` assets (like heat pumps or electrolyzers), ESSIM uses the price profiles of the input and output carriers to determine if a conversion is economically viable at the current time step.
    - It calculates the weighted sum of input carrier prices and compares it with the weighted sum of output carrier prices (based on the conversion efficiencies/ratios).
    - If the output value is less than the input cost, the conversion node will generate a flat bid curve (0 demand/supply), effectively disabling the conversion for that time step.
    - This behavior can be disabled by setting the environment variable `DISABLE_PRICE_BASED_BID_DECISIONS=true`.
2.  **Default Marginal Cost**: If a `Producer` or `Consumer` asset does not have a specific marginal cost profile defined in its `CostInformation`, ESSIM uses the price profile of the connected carrier as the default marginal cost.

## Asset Bidding Logic

Each asset in a network generates a **Bid Function** (or Bid Curve) that relates market price to energy demand (positive for consumption, negative for production).

### Producers
- **Inflexible Production**: Assets with a fixed energy profile (e.g., wind, solar) bid their full production at all price levels.
- **Adjustable Production**: Assets without a profile (e.g., dispatchable generators) bid based on their **Marginal Cost**. They will offer production if the market price is above their marginal cost.

### Consumers
- **Inflexible Consumption**: Assets with a fixed demand profile bid their full consumption at all price levels.
- **Adjustable Consumption**: Assets without a profile bid based on their **Marginal Cost**. They will consume if the market price is below their marginal cost.

### Storage
Storage assets (e.g., batteries) typically have a two-step bid curve:
- **Charging**: If the market price is below the `mcCharge` (Marginal Charge Cost), the storage acts as a consumer.
- **Discharging**: If the market price is above the `mcDischarge` (Marginal Discharge Cost), the storage acts as a producer.
- **Idle**: Between these two price levels, the storage remains idle.

## Transport Solver and Matching Price

The `TransportSolver` for a specific carrier network performs the following steps:

1.  **Aggregation**: It collects and sums up all bid curves from all assets in the network.
2.  **Equilibrium**: It finds the **Matching Price** (or balancing price) where the net demand in the network is zero (or as close to zero as possible).
3.  **Allocation**: Based on this Matching Price, it determines the final assigned energy for each asset.
4.  **Imbalance**: If no exact equilibrium can be found within the transport constraints, the remaining net demand is recorded as an imbalance.

## Observations and KPIs

The results of the market balancing are recorded as observations:
- **`MATCHING_PRICE`**: The equilibrium price found for the network.
- **`IMBALANCE_ENERGY`**: The remaining energy imbalance in the network.
- **Asset Costs**: The cost or revenue for each asset is often calculated based on the assigned energy and either the Matching Price or the carrier's exogenous price profile.
